The Best Time to Visit Japan: An Honest Month-by-Month Guide

Spring cherry blossoms and autumn foliage are spectacular. They are also the two most crowded periods of the year. Here is what every month actually offers.

In this article

  1. January–February: winter, crowds at minimum
  2. March–April: cherry blossoms
  3. May: the sweet spot
  4. June–August: summer and humidity
  5. September–November: autumn foliage
  6. The recommendation

January–February: winter, crowds at minimum

January is Japan's quietest month for tourism. After New Year's (January 1–3, busy with domestic travel), the country empties. Hotels are cheaper. Shinkansen seats are available. Famous sites are accessible at human scale. The weather in Tokyo and Kyoto is cold but manageable — 4–10°C, mostly clear. Onsen towns are at their peak: Hakone, Kinosaki, Beppu. February adds Sapporo's Snow Festival (first week), one of Japan's most-attended winter events. Good months for people who want the experience without the crowd premium.

March–April: cherry blossoms

Sakura season typically runs late March to mid-April depending on location and year. Tokyo usually peaks in late March; Kyoto about a week later; northern Japan and higher elevations into late April. The flowers last around one to two weeks at any given location. This is the most visited season, the most expensive, and the most beautiful. Book accommodation four to six months in advance. The parks and famous viewpoints are genuinely crowded. The less-visited spots — riverside embankments, school paths, neighborhood parks — offer the same flowers with a fraction of the people.

May: the sweet spot

Late May (after Golden Week, which runs April 29–May 5) is one of the best times to visit Japan. Spring is still present. Temperatures are comfortable — 15–22°C in most of central Japan. The golden week crowds have gone. Hotels return to normal pricing. The greenery is at its freshest. May 10–30 is consistently underrated.

June–August: summer and humidity

June brings the rainy season (tsuyu) to most of Honshu — two to four weeks of grey skies and intermittent rain that is frustrating for outdoor sightseeing. July and August are hot and humid: 30–36°C in Tokyo and Osaka with 80%+ humidity. Summer festivals (matsuri) are spectacular but the weather is punishing. Hokkaido is the exception — cool, green, and genuinely pleasant in July and August. If you must visit in summer, either go to Hokkaido or plan your days around early mornings and air-conditioned afternoons.

September–November: autumn foliage

September brings typhoon season risk (primarily September–October) and the beginning of more comfortable temperatures. By October the weather is excellent: clear days, 15–25°C, minimal rain. Autumn foliage (koyo) peaks in November — Hokkaido in early October, central Honshu through November. The period from late October through mid-November is the second most visited after cherry blossom season, and prices reflect this. Kyoto in November is busy to the point of requiring strategic planning around early mornings and weekdays.

The recommendation

For a first visit with no fixed seasonal preference: late May or mid-October. For a visit specifically structured around a natural event: cherry blossoms in late March–early April (plan far ahead), autumn leaves in early-mid November. For minimum crowds and maximum value: January or June (accepting the weather trade-offs of each). The worst time to book without planning is Golden Week (late April–early May) or the first week of August. Not because they're bad — but because accommodation disappears a year in advance and prices reflect demand.
